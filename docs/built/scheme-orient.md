# scheme:orient (plan-14)

Built: `scheme:orient` turns each structure after an arrow onto the structure before it, by the core they share. One undo step; no new operation (it emits `flip` and `rotate`, which have exact inverses). **Run orient, then row** (`scheme:row` places, `scheme:orient` only turns): orient then row is stable (a second round changes nothing, tested); row then orient may leave a layout that a further row moves a little, because the turn changes the box (it never undoes the turn, tested).

- **Module**: `crates/mzdraw/src/orient.rs` (`plan`, `solve`, `orient`). Kept apart from plan-13's `scheme.rs`: that file is another slice's and the two commands share only `commands.rs`. The roles come from the one rule of `to_rxn` and `scheme:row` (`rxn::along`, `rxn::role`), not from a rule of its own.
- **Pairs**: for each structure, the middle of the structure along each arrow gives its role (`rxn::role`): reactant (before the tail), product (past the head) or agent (alongside, at any distance: an agent is never turned and never a source). A structure is a reactant of the arrow whose tail is nearest and a product of the arrow whose head is nearest. Each product is oriented on its nearest reactants first (at most 8 tried), the first with a core: A + B -> C orients C on A or on B, whichever has a core. A reactant that is a product of an earlier arrow is turned first (A -> B -> C turns B on A, then C on the turned B). An arrow with both anchors (`from`, `to`) uses its two structures instead. A product is oriented once. At most 200 products per command; the rest are reported `too_large`.
- **Core**: `SubQuery::matches` of `mzlab-mol` (public at the pinned commit): the smaller structure in the larger one; when that fails, the ring systems (non-bridge bonds, linear lowlink search; the 4 largest of each side) of either in the other. At least 3 heavy atoms. Hydrogens drawn as atoms are left out of the query.
- **Fit**: 2D Kabsch with `libm`. Every match is fitted; the least error wins, among equals the smallest turn. A mirror (`flip` about the vertical axis through the box centre of the target) is tried only when the target has no wedge, hash or wave bond, and kept only when it lowers the error by more than 1e-3. A core that does not fit as a rigid piece (rms above 0.05 bond lengths: drawn bent, or only a mirror fits a target with a wedge) counts as no core.
- **Place**: the turn is about the centre of the box of the target; nothing is moved (E28: placing is the layout's). **Core atoms keep their exact coordinates only if the target is turned about the core**; here the pivot is the box centre, so the core of the target ends oriented like the source's, not at its place. The rms in the tests and in the report is with the shift taken away. The route of anchored arrows follows.
- **Size**: a structure of more than 500 heavy atoms is not searched (`too_large`, `error.draw2.orientSize`); a search of more than 100,000 steps (identical copies of a grid are the worst case: every row and column matches) stops and says `too_large` too, not "no core". Measured (debug build): two identical 16 x 16 grids (256 atoms) are oriented, 20 x 20 (400 atoms) stop at the search limit; 3600 atoms per structure are refused in under a second; a drawing of 6000 pieces is planned in under a second (the tests bound these at 5 and 10 s). The cost is bounded by the sizes above, not by the drawing (the earlier build was cubic in the atoms of a piece).
- **Overlap** (refused, nothing changed, `error.draw2.orientOverlap`, atomic: no pair is applied): after the turn, an atom of the target closer than 0.3 bond lengths to an atom it was not that close to before; or the box of the target meets the box of another structure, or of an arrow, plus sign or text (0.3 reach; an arrow anchored to a structure is left out, its ends follow it), that it did not meet before. A text has no width here (a point), so a long text may still be run into.
- **Refusals**: `orientNone` (no product has a core), `orientSize`, `orientOverlap`. **Not a refusal**: a scheme that is oriented already (also `orient` twice): the command does nothing, the live line says "already oriented", the result has `changed: false`.
- **Result document** (`draw-result/1`, only when a command was `scheme:orient`): `changed` (bool) and `orient`: one item per product `{arrow, from, to, status, core, flip, deg, before, after[, at]}`, `status` in `oriented`, `already`, `no_core`, `too_large`, `overlap`; the items of the last `scheme:orient` command. Schema row in `docs/schemas/draw-result-1.json`, `docs/mzdraw.md`.
- **CLI twin**: `{"command":"scheme:orient"}` (no `args`; any argument is an invalid request, exit 2); the log holds `flip`/`rotate` and the route operations. Page: button `scheme-orient` in the edit palette. Same bytes (`tests/orient.rs`, golden `tests/data/log/scheme-orient.json`, parity).
- **Independent proof**: `tools/fidelity/orient.py` against RDKit `GenerateDepictionMatching2DStructure`; committed output `tools/fidelity/orient.reference.json` (RDKit 2026.09.2, 2026-11-21: 4 cases compared, 0 skipped, worst difference 0.006 degrees; limit 2). The script skips, and prints the count of, cases where the source is not an exact substructure of the product (RDKit has no reference there). Weak by construction: its reference puts the core on the source's, which is the aim of the command; the independent part is the angle of the core, not the choice of the core.

Not built:
- `scheme:orient-series` (E18): a later row of its own, not part of this slice.
- A maximum-common-substructure engine. Cases for COO 16 (both are tested to give `no_core`, `tests/orient.rs`):
  1. A shared chain on two different rings: propylbenzene `CCCc1ccccc1` -> propylcyclohexane `CCCC1CCCCC1`. Expected core: the propyl chain with its ring carbon (4 atoms), the target turned so that the chain lies as the source's (angle of the chain axis equal within 2 degrees).
  2. Two ring systems that share a part: indane `C1Cc2ccccc2C1` -> tetralin `C1CCc2ccccc2C1`. Expected core: the benzene ring (6 atoms), the target turned so that the ring and its two fusion atoms lie as the source's.
- Per-structure text width of a `text` object in the overlap test.
- A keyboard shortcut (the button is reached with Tab).
- Abbreviation labels match by the atoms of their expansion; a pair that differs only by a label is not tested.
