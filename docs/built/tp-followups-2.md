## 2. The gap rule `crossing` for secondary and skipping arrows (tp-05b follow-up)
**The rule.** After the rules `slope`, `length`, `label` and the primary `crossing` have set every gap of a component, `pathway::long_gaps` looks at the arrows that are not primary (a secondary arrow, which may join adjacent layers, or one that skips a layer; a primary arrow always joins adjacent layers). Each such arrow, clipped at the route boxes of its two ends, is tested against the route box (box plus `pathway.margin`) of every other structure in the columns it spans, ends included. An arrow that meets a box widens, by one `pathway::CROSSING_STEP`, the gaps that move it off the box (below); all arrows of a round widen together, and the round is repeated, at most `CROSSING_STEPS` (400) rounds, so a gap grows by at most 20 bond lengths.
**Which gaps.** For the first box the arrow meets (lowest layer first, then the box centre nearest the arrow, then the smaller index): a box in an end column is cleared by a flatter arrow, so every gap the arrow spans grows; a box in a layer between is cleared by moving the height of the arrow at that layer away from the box centre, which is a wider part before the layer (the arrow is nearer its end) or after it (nearer its start). The choice is the sign of (rise of the arrow) x (arrow minus box centre), which is the same for an arrow and its mirror image in y; a horizontal arrow (rise 0) or one through the box centre has no side, so every spanned gap grows, and boxes equally near the arrow in the first layer it meets (one above, one below) all count, so no index decides. The choice itself is `pathway::widen_range`, tested alone for every side, rise, tie and end column (`the_gaps_to_grow_are_the_same_for_an_arrow_and_its_mirror_image`; run against the first version of the rule, which took one box and had no horizontal case, it fails: left `0..1`, right `0..3`). The rule is symmetric: tested on the rule alone for each branch (horizontal arrow with a box above, below and both; the gap before the box; the gap after it; equal distance) and on layouts, where `order=reverse-id` lays a DAG out as the mirror image of the one by id (compared where both have the same forest of primary arrows, since the choice of primary parent among parents of one layer follows the order); the gaps of a round are the union of the spans of all arrows, so the result does not depend on the order of the arrows (both tested on the rule alone).
**At the bound.** Nothing is promised that a gap can do: an arrow in a straight chain passes the middle box whatever the gaps are. The state kept is the first round with the fewest arrows still meeting a box (round 0, the gaps as they were, is a candidate), so the rule never leaves more crossings than it found. What still meets a box is the keyed finding `std.scheme.gapLimit` (`std/1` shows one finding per component, `objects` the arrows still meeting a box; the per-gap notes, with the long arrows that span each gap and still meet a box, are in `Layout::limits`; the text of its row in the request table says so) and, as always, `std.scheme.crossing`. A widened gap is reported as `std.scheme.gapRaised` with rule `crossing`; its `arrows` are now all arrows that span the gap.
**What it did** on the 199 seeded random DAGs of 5 to 12 structures tried while building it: 284 crossings before, 189 after (the others are the cases no gap clears, each reported as `gapLimit`). The DAG of `tests/data/pathways/dag.json` keeps its one crossing (the arrow 0 -> 3 stays at the bound; the test that pins it is unchanged). Goldens that changed (checked against the files of `main`, case by case and output by output): **layout moved** in `tp-import.json` (two cases: the convergent TP is laid out with a wider gap, so coordinates, files and `std` change) and in `tp-table.json` (one case, the SDF carries the moved coordinates); **only the `std` output changed** in `pathway.json` and `pathway-page.json` (the layout is unchanged and the crossing stays: `std.scheme.gapLimit` is added) and in `pathway-label.json` (the case does not lay out; its `std` now reports `std.scheme.gapRaised` where it reported `std.scheme.crossing`: the layout `pathway:tree` would make is clean). New cases in `pathway-gap.json`; `edit-schema.json` has the new text of the `gapLimit` row.
**Cost.** The columns are sorted by y once; a test of one arrow costs O(log P + hits) per column it spans (the same binary search as the primary rule, now shared as `near`), so a round costs O(E_long x span x (log P + hits)) and the rule at most 401 rounds. `scaling_the_long_rule_alone_is_linear_in_the_arrows` builds columns where one arrow is never cleared (so all 401 rounds run) and asserts, on the thread CPU clock (`tests/support/timing.rs`, best of seven, no floor, no wall-clock figure), that four times the arrows cost less than seven times the time. The caps (1000 structures or arrows, a cycle) are checked before any of this.
**Tests** (`tests/pathway_gap.rs`): a secondary arrow and skipping arrows widen the gaps they span and leave no crossing; a chain with a skipping arrow no gap clears gives the limit on both gaps and the crossing, with the gaps as they were; over 80 seeded DAGs no primary arrow grazes and every crossing is named by a `gapLimit`; two runs of a layout are equal in centres, gaps, limits, crossings and operations (30 DAGs under three settings); the rule alone widens the gap before the box for a rising arrow, is equal for a mirror image in y (every branch) and for any order of the arrows; a layout and its `reverse-id` twin have the same gaps, limits and crossings; golden cases wasm == native for a secondary arrow, skipping arrows and the unclearable chain.
