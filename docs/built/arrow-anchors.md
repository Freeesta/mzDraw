# Arrows that know their structures, built

(tp-01; `crates/mzdraw/src/route.rs`, `commands.rs`; rows in `schema.rs`; tests `crates/mzdraw/tests/anchors.rs`, golden cases `tests/data/log/anchors.json`.) An `arrow` object of `mzdraw/1` may carry `from` and `to`, the id of one atom of the structure (connected piece) it leaves and of the one it reaches; absent or `0` is a free end, and a document without anchors is written byte for byte as before. `obj.add` and `obj.set` take `from` and `to` (`obj.set` with `0` frees the end; the inverse is the whole object as before; a plus or a text with an anchor is refused with `error.draw2.object`); the four op-table rows carry `mzlab: false` until mzLab adopts them (COO 16). Removing an anchored atom never rewrites the anchor: it is kept as written and used as a free end, and `std/1` gets one `warn` finding `std.scheme.anchor` that lists those arrows (`objects`). `route::route(doc, arrow, margin, font)` gives a `move` of the arrow for the tail and then an `obj.set` of `x2`, `y2` (none when already there): the routed ends are the segment between the centres of the two structure boxes, clipped at each box inflated by the new setting `style.pathway.margin` (0.35 bond lengths, `settings::FIELDS`, key `pathway.margin`). **Follow is a command behaviour:** `Editor::following` (called by `Editor::run` and by the key nudges, the one place) appends `route::follow` to the ops of any editor command, computed on the document after the command, for each anchored arrow whose structure box changed, in the same undo step; the log carries the ops, so a native replay of the page's log gives the page's bytes (tested for `Alt+Arrow`). A bare `{"op":"move"}` in a request's `log` stays a bare move. The request has `commands` (`[{"command","args"}]`, after `log`, before `out`) and the singular `command` + `args` (a list of one; both together is `error.draw2.request`); the commands are `move`, `rotate`, `flip` with the fields of the op of the same name (`rotate` without `x`, `y` and `flip` without `at` work about the centre of the atoms). A refused command stops the request: with `report`, `dry_run` or `expect` it is `draw-result/1` with `failed.stage` `command` and `failed.op` the index in `commands`, a bare request returns the key as an error; an unknown command name is an invalid request (`error.draw2.command`); the rows `commands`, `command`, `args` are in `schema::REQUEST` (`"out":"schema"`, section `request`). The anchors are kept only in `mzdraw/1` (`save("mzdraw")`, the pictures that carry the document); KET, CDXML and RXN have no such field.

The box of a structure is its atoms plus the ink of their labels, taken from `scene::label_extent`, which lays the label out as the painter does (a group label with its attachment atom on the point and the rest on the side away from its bond; hydrogens, mass number, charge and the lowered digits where `Painter::label` puts them). The vertical reach is a line of text (0.6 of the font each way, more for stacked hydrogens, raised marks and lowered digits), not the glyph outlines, and the horizontal reach uses the same width table as the painter, so the box can be a little wider than the ink and never narrower: a test paints group labels (CO2Et, Ph, NMe2, OMe, Boc, R1) on either side of their bond, with hydrogens, charge and mass number, and checks that every piece of painted ink lies inside the box of its structure. The clip is checked against a brute-force sampler (10,000 points, 200 seeded box pairs) and the follow property on seeded random move, rotate and flip commands.

Not built: the arrow tool, `pathway:anchor` and `pathway:route`, the page (tp-02); the velocity task "parent with three TPs and arrows; move the parent" (it needs the page path of tp-02); layout and labels (tp-05, tp-06, tp-07); polyline arrows; the plan-13 `scheme:row` route (plan-13 has not merged).
