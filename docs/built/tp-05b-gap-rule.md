# Pathway layout: the gap rule `crossing` (tp-05b), built

(`crates/mzdraw/src/pathway.rs` step 5; tests `crates/mzdraw/tests/pathway_gap.rs` and `tests/pathway.rs`; golden cases `tests/data/log/pathway-gap.json`; independent check `tools/fidelity/pathway_props.py`.)

**The rule.** After the rules `slope`, `length` and `label` of tp-05 have set the gap `g_k` between the layers `k` and `k + 1` of a component, `pathway::crossing_gap` checks the primary arrows between those two layers on the boxes of the two columns. If one of them meets the route box (box plus `pathway.margin`) of another structure of the two layers, `g_k` grows in steps of `pathway::CROSSING_STEP` (0.05 bond lengths) until none does, at most `pathway::CROSSING_STEPS` (400) steps, so it adds at most 20 bond lengths to a gap. A gap it widened is reported as `std.scheme.gapRaised` as before (rule `crossing` in `Layout::gaps`). **At the bound** (400 steps and still an arrow meets a box) the gap is left as it was, `Layout::limits` holds the gap and the keyed finding `std.scheme.gapLimit` (warn; `atoms` the first root of the component, `objects` the primary arrows between the two layers; English text `draw2.std.scheme.gapLimit`; row in `schema::REQUEST`) says so, and the arrows that still meet a box are listed as `std.scheme.crossing` as always (tested on a fan of 300 at `max_angle` 89, which the rule cannot clear). The new key is a row of the request table, so the golden of the table of operations (`tests/data/log/edit-schema.json`) gained that row. No new operation, setting or command; the page paints the same moves, so the page, `pathway:tree` and the native replay give the same bytes (`tests/data/log/pathway-gap.json`, parity).

**Cost.** The rule is its own function so that its cost is tested alone. The two columns are sorted by y once, O(P log P); a check of one gap value tests each arrow only against the boxes whose y range meets the arrow's own range inside the x slab of a column (a binary search), O(E (log P + hits)), and stops at the first arrow that meets a box; the rule makes at most 401 checks. So the work is at most 401 times that, plus the sort, for each pair of layers. The degenerate worst case is an arrow that meets very many boxes: that one arrow costs O(P) per check, and the check stops at it. The default fan of 999 never reaches the loop (the slope rule already clears it); the case that does run all 401 checks is the bound itself. `scaling_the_rule_alone_at_its_bound_is_linear_in_the_arrows` builds columns where every check runs through all the clean arrows before the last one fails, and asserts that four times the arrows cost less than seven times the time (best of three, no floor, debug and release); replacing the binary search by a scan of the whole column makes it fail (ratio 16 in a mutation run). The caps (1000 structures or arrows, a cycle) are checked before any of this (`the_caps_are_checked_before_the_work`).

**The page limit.** The rule adds at most 20 bond lengths to a gap, so it cannot push a layout off the page by itself (a fan of 999 at `max_angle` 1 has a slope gap near 50,000 and is still laid out; the limit of a coordinate is `MAX_COORD`, 1e6). A layout that does leave the page is refused by `pathway::ops` with the document's own key and nothing changes, as before (`Refusal::Apply`, tested in `tests/pathway.rs`). The rule does not clamp a gap to the page.

**What it fixes.** `star5` (2 crossings) and `big200` (3) at `max_angle` 40 are clean now (P12 holds for every tree of the synthetic set). The fans of 3 to 12 leaves are clean at `max_angle` 40, 60, 75 and `dir=down` (tested).

**Properties tested** (`tests/pathway_gap.rs`): a layout is deterministic (centres, gaps and operations equal on two runs, 40 random trees under four settings); no two clearance boxes overlap on those trees; no primary arrow meets a route box; a gap is not widened when nothing grazes; the bound case above; the English text and the schema row of the new key.

**Not built.** Crossings inside a column are not addressed (they stay reported as `std.scheme.crossing`). Secondary arrows and arrows that skip a layer are now widened for: see `tp-followups-2.md`. The default step 0.05 and the bound 400 are decisions of this slice, not of the specification.
