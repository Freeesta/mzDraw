# Group labels reversed on the left side, built

(arch-std-08): a group label that grows to the left of its atom (the bonds leave to the right) is drawn with its units in the reverse order, so that the attachment atom faces the bond: `CO2H` as `HO2C`, `OMe` as `MeO`, `NMe2` as `Me2N`, `NO2` as `O2N`, `CF3` as `F3C`, `SO3H` as `HO3S`, `CO2Me` as `MeO2C`. `label::arrange` reads a label as units (an element symbol with its count, an abbreviation of the table with its count, an `R` with its number, a group in parentheses with its count) and writes them in either order, each unit keeping its own; an abbreviation is one unit (`Boc`, `Ph`, `Ac`, `Me` are never split and a label that is one unit is unchanged), a count stays after its symbol, and a text that is not made of units is drawn as written. The side is the one the hydrogens already use (`Painter::grows_left`: the sum of the bond directions); with no bond, or bonds that cancel out or go straight up or down, the written form stays. The attachment atom is the one centred on the atom of the drawing (the symbol, not its count: `R` of `R1`); a bond stops short of what is drawn beyond it on its side (the `1` of `R1`; `Painter::clear` now covers group labels). One code path in `scene.rs`, so the canvas and the SVG agree, and the replay request (`mzlab-cli mol draw` route) gives the same bytes; no new operation, the document keeps the label as written. `CO2H` joined the table as a synonym of `COOH` (same expansion). Covered by `tests/reversal.rs`, a case of the golden log (`tests/data/log/labels.json`), two labelled structures on the flaw sheet and the e2e steps (keyboard, mouse, touch; screenshots in `docs/shots/`). Three golden SVGs moved by one text line each (`OMe` became `MeO`: the presets `acs-1996` and `slides` and the settings given in place). Not built: charges moved off a crowded corner, expansion of abbreviations on the canvas, free-text labels (a label is still one of the table or `R#`), the reversal of the hydrogens of an element label (`H2N` was already written that way).
