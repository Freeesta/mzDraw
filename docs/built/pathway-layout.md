# Pathway layout: arrows that organise themselves (tp-05), built

(`crates/mzdraw/src/pathway.rs`; tests `crates/mzdraw/tests/pathway.rs`; synthetic pathways `tests/data/pathways/*.json`, written by `tools/gen_pathways.py`; golden cases `tests/data/log/pathway.json`; independent check `tools/fidelity/pathway_props.py`.)

**The command.** `pathway:tree` (an editor command of a request, `{"commands":[{"command":"pathway:tree"}]}`, no `args`) lays out the whole graph of the anchored arrows of tp-01 as one undo step. A node is a structure that is the `from` or `to` of an anchored arrow joining two different structures; an edge is that arrow. Steps, as the specification states them: layers (longest directed path from a root), one primary parent per node (largest layer, then the order key, then the smallest atom id), children and roots in the order key, the tidy tree of Buchheim, Jünger and Leipert (2002) over the box heights with `pathway.row_gap` between neighbours, one column x per layer and component with the gap between columns raised above `pathway.col_gap` only where a primary arrow would be steeper than `pathway.max_angle` (rule `slope`) or shorter than `pathway.min_arrow` after clipping (rule `length`), the first root of the first component fixed and later components stacked below at `2 * row_gap` with their first root at the same x. The operations are the rounded `move`s (by the smallest atom id of the structure), then tp-01's routing operations of every edge by arrow id, computed on the moved document; no new operation, so the log of `draw_edit/1` replays to the same bytes natively and in the `.wasm` (`tests/parity.mjs`, three cases in `tests/data/log/pathway.json`). `pathway::layout(doc, style)` gives the unrounded positions and the report, `pathway::ops` the operations, `pathway::command` both; `Editor::run_command` runs a command for a Rust caller.

**Refusals** (exit 1, `failed.stage` `command`, nothing changes): `error.draw2.schemeCycle` (`failed.id` the smallest arrow id on a cycle), `error.draw2.schemeDuplicate` (the smallest arrow id of the pair), `error.draw2.schemeEmpty` (id 0).

**Settings** `pathway.dir` (`right`, `down`), `col_gap` 2.5, `row_gap` 1, `max_angle` 40, `min_arrow` 1.2, `order` (`id`, `mz`, `rt`; `reverse-id` exists for the mirror test), `margin` 0.35, by `{"set":["pathway.col_gap=3"]}` (`Editor::set`, section `pathway`). The four numbers are rows of `settings::FIELDS` (so they are in the settings file, `style.pathway.*`); `dir` and `order` are written only when not the default. The settings files of the golden `settings.json` gained those four keys.

**Outputs.** `"out":"boxes"` gives `boxes/1`: every structure (smallest atom id, label-inclusive box) and every arrow with the structures it joins, at 4 decimals. `"out":"std"` now also lists the layout's findings when the drawing has a pathway: `std.scheme.gapRaised` (info; `atoms` the first root of the component, `objects` the primary arrows between the two layers), `std.scheme.free` (info; the arrows without two structures, the plus signs and the texts, which are not moved), `std.scheme.crossing` (warn; the arrow and the structures it meets). A crossing is a visible segment through the route box (box plus `pathway.margin`) of another *node*; structures that are not nodes are not looked at. The rows are in `schema::REQUEST` (`"out":"schema"`).

**Proof.** `tests/pathway.rs` checks P1 to P14 on 13 synthetic pathways (chain, stars of 2 to 5, a star ordered by `MZ` and `RT`, binary tree of depth 3, unbalanced tree, DAG with a convergent TP and a long edge, two components, mixed sizes, a messy star, 200 nodes) on the unrounded positions (1e-9) and on the saved document (3e-4), also with `dir=down` and other settings; P8 on every tree, P14 on a three-component document built in the test; each refusal; undo returns the identical document; the messy star fails P6, P11 and P12 as committed. The plain Buchheim algorithm holds P7, P8, P10 and P13 on every test tree, so the two-pass mean of the specification is **not** used and P13 is claimed in full. `tools/fidelity/pathway_props.py` re-derives the graph, layers and forest from `boxes/1` and re-checks P5, P6, P7, P9, P10, P11, P12 and P8 without any Rust shared (13 of 13). 200 nodes lay out in about 2 ms in release (printed by the test; asserted below 100 ms only in release).

**Where the text of the specification and the result differ.**
- P3: the moves are byte for byte the same under a translation on the grid; the `obj.set` of an arrow carries the absolute head `x2`, `y2`, which moves by the same (a, b). The ops are not byte-identical as a whole, and cannot be without a new operation.
- P12: "none for the trees of equal box sizes" is not true at the default slope: in a fan of five (`star5`) the two outer arrows graze the route box of their neighbour at the head (2 crossings), and the random 200-node tree has 3; the DAG's long edge has 1. The report is exact (a sampler agrees on every pathway) and each pathway's test states its count. A rule that raises the gap until no primary arrow meets a neighbour's route box would remove them; it is a change of step 5 and is left to the coordinator.
- A displacement that is a tie on the 4-decimal grid goes toward zero (box centres often sit on a half step), which is what makes P4 hold.

**Not built.** The page button, the e2e test and the Graphviz proof (tp-06: this slice has no UI, so no e2e and no screenshots); polyline routing, lane pins, a y axis by Δm/z or RT; labels on arrows (tp-07, so `g_k` leaves no room for a label yet); structures that are not nodes are not moved or avoided. The preprint of Buchheim et al. was not read; the module follows Walker's published description as made linear by them. Patent clearance: unverified.
