# Pathway layout: arrows that organise themselves (tp-05), built

(`crates/mzdraw/src/pathway.rs`; tests `crates/mzdraw/tests/pathway.rs`; synthetic pathways `tests/data/pathways/*.json`, written by `tools/gen_pathways.py`; golden cases `tests/data/log/pathway.json`; independent check `tools/fidelity/pathway_props.py`.)

**The command.** `pathway:tree` (an editor command of a request, `{"commands":[{"command":"pathway:tree"}]}`, no `args`) lays out the whole graph of the anchored arrows of tp-01 as one undo step. A node is a structure that is the `from` or `to` of an anchored arrow joining two different structures; an edge is that arrow. Steps, as the specification states them: layers (longest directed path from a root), one primary parent per node (largest layer, then the order key, then the smallest atom id), children and roots in the order key, the tidy tree of Buchheim, Jünger and Leipert (2002) over the box heights with `pathway.row_gap` between neighbours, one column x per layer and component with the gap between columns raised above `pathway.col_gap` only where a primary arrow would be steeper than `pathway.max_angle` (rule `slope`) or shorter than `pathway.min_arrow` after clipping (rule `length`), the first root of the first component fixed and later components stacked below at `2 * row_gap` with their first root at the same x. The operations are the rounded `move`s (by the smallest atom id of the structure), then tp-01's routing operations of every edge by arrow id, computed on the moved document; no new operation, so the log of `draw_edit/1` replays to the same bytes natively and in the `.wasm` (`tests/parity.mjs`, three cases in `tests/data/log/pathway.json`). `pathway::layout(doc, style)` gives the unrounded positions and the report, `pathway::ops` the operations, `pathway::command` both; `Editor::run_command` runs a command for a Rust caller.

**Refusals** (exit 1, `failed.stage` `command`, nothing changes): `error.draw2.schemeSize` (more than `pathway::MAX_ARROWS` = 1000 anchored arrows or `MAX_NODES` = 1000 structures, id 0; checked before any walk, so a hostile document is refused, never walked: the walks are recursive and 1000 deep at most; in the `.wasm` a chain of 1000 lays out in 145 ms and chains of 1001, 8000 and 20000 are refused, the 20000 one in 8.6 s of which the import of the document itself is 8.1 s), `error.draw2.schemeCycle` (`failed.id` the smallest arrow id on a cycle), `error.draw2.schemeDuplicate` (the smallest arrow id of the pair), `error.draw2.schemeEmpty` (id 0).

**Settings** `pathway.dir` (`right`, `down`), `col_gap` 2.5, `row_gap` 1, `max_angle` 40, `min_arrow` 1.2, `order` (`id`, `mz`, `rt`; `reverse-id` exists for the mirror test), `margin` 0.35, by `{"set":["pathway.col_gap=3"]}` (`Editor::set`, section `pathway`). The four numbers are rows of `settings::FIELDS` (so they are in the settings file, `style.pathway.*`); `dir` and `order` are written only when not the default. The settings files of the golden `settings.json` gained those four keys.

**Outputs.** `"out":"boxes"` gives `boxes/1`: every structure (smallest atom id, label-inclusive box) and every arrow with the structures it joins, at 4 decimals. `"out":"std"`, and the `report` and `expect` arms of `draw-result/1` (`std`, `std_absent`), now also see the layout's findings when the drawing has a pathway: `std.scheme.gapRaised` (info; `atoms` the first root of the component, `objects` the primary arrows between the two layers), `std.scheme.free` (info; the arrows without two structures, the plus signs and the texts, which are not moved), `std.scheme.crossing` (warn; the arrow and the structures it meets). A crossing is a visible segment through the route box (box plus `pathway.margin`) of another *node*; structures that are not nodes are not looked at. The rows are in `schema::REQUEST` (`"out":"schema"`).

**Proof.** `tests/pathway.rs` checks P1 to P14 on 13 synthetic pathways (chain, stars of 2 to 5, a star ordered by `MZ` and `RT`, binary tree of depth 3, unbalanced tree, DAG with a convergent TP and a long edge, two components, mixed sizes, a messy star, 200 nodes) on the unrounded positions (1e-9) and on the saved document (3e-4), also with `dir=down` and other settings; P8 on every tree, P14 on a three-component document built in the test; each refusal; undo returns the identical document; the messy star fails P6, P11 and P12 as committed. The plain Buchheim algorithm holds P7, P8, P10 and P13 on every test tree, so the two-pass mean of the specification is **not** used and P13 is claimed in full. `tools/fidelity/pathway_props.py` re-derives the graph, layers and forest from `boxes/1` and re-checks P5, P6, P7, P9, P10, P11, P12 and P8 without any Rust shared (13 of 13). 200 nodes lay out in about 2 ms in release (printed by the test; asserted below 100 ms only in release).

A self-loop (an anchored arrow whose two ends are in one structure) is not an edge: it is listed as free, and a document with only such arrows is `schemeEmpty`. `order=reverse-id` is session-only (`--set pathway.order=reverse-id`, for the mirror test P8): it is not written to a settings file, a file that holds it is refused, and it is not in the public schema row. `tools/fidelity/pathway_props.py` needs the debug replay example built (`cargo build -p mzdraw --example replay`) and checks the default settings only; without the binary it prints `skipped` on stderr and exits 0. The only page change is that the style panel, generated from `settings::FIELDS`, shows the four new numbers (screenshot at deviceScaleFactor 2: `docs/screenshots/tp-05-style-panel.png`); `tests/e2e.mjs` has one step for them (defaults, set by touch and by keyboard, a value out of bounds refused with its key, the language texts) next to the existing assertion that the panel holds exactly the fields of the schema.

**Cost and live checks.** In release, 1000 nodes (a chain, the largest allowed): layout 65 ms, ops 12 ms, the `std` report 73 ms (printed by `a_thousand_nodes_in_time_...`); 200 nodes: 2 ms. Because 73 ms is more than a live check should take, the page's live check after each edit (`recheck`, and the selection of a finding from its panel) does **not** run the pathway layout; the findings are in `out: std`, `report` and `expect`. `pathway::ops` returns `Refusal::Apply(key, id)` (the key of the document's own refusal, e.g. a coordinate past the page) instead of an empty list when the moves are refused; nothing changes (tested). Parity runs a chain of 1000 structures (dry run, laid out) and one of 1001 (`schemeSize`) in the `.wasm` and natively (`tests/data/log/pathway.json`). `std.scheme.free` lists the plus signs and texts of any drawing that has an anchored arrow, so the golden `scheme-row.json` (plan-13) moved by one `info` finding on its plus signs and texts: that is the specification's report and it is wanted.

**Where the text of the specification and the result differ.**
- P3: what is byte-identical under a translation by (a, b) on the 4-decimal grid: every `move` of a structure (same atoms, same `dx`, `dy`), the number and order of the operations. What is not: the `obj.set` of an arrow carries the absolute head `x2`, `y2`, which moves by the same (a, b) (tested: the heads are the originals plus (a, b), 1e-9); the saved atoms are the originals plus (a, b). The ops as a whole therefore cannot be byte-identical without a new operation. Undo is exact: `pathway:tree` is one undo step and undo returns the identical document (tested on all 13 pathways).
- P12, "no crossings for trees of equal box sizes": holds for the chain, the stars of 2, 3 and 4, the `MZ` star, the binary tree, the unbalanced tree and the two components; **fails for `star5` (2 crossings) and `big200` (3) at `max_angle` 40**. Cause: the steepest arrows of a fan graze the route box of the neighbouring box at their head. The DAG's one crossing is its long edge (layer 0 to 2) passing a box. The report is exact (a sampler agrees on every pathway). Fixed since by the gap rule of step 5 (tp-05b, `docs/built/tp-05b-gap-rule.md`): `star5` and `big200` have no crossing now; the DAG's one remains.
- A displacement that is a tie on the 4-decimal grid goes toward zero (box centres often sit on a half step), which is what makes P4 hold.

**Not built.** The page button, the page e2e test of the command and the Graphviz proof (tp-06; this slice adds no control for the command); polyline routing, lane pins, a y axis by Δm/z or RT; labels on arrows (tp-07, so `g_k` leaves no room for a label yet); structures that are not nodes are not moved or avoided. The paper of Buchheim, Jünger and Leipert (2002) was not read: it is cited only as the name of the algorithm; Walker (1990) was not read either, and the code is written from general knowledge of the algorithm, checked by the properties; the module follows Walker's published description as made linear by them.
